## 0. The decision in one paragraph

The method line is closed and the paper is not a method paper. It is a **measurement paper with
a reusable protocol, a calibrated reference-quality gate, and a released labelled dataset**. For
a released frozen humanoid motion prior (ARDY-G1 Horizon52) driven through its native interface
we measure, before anyone builds a planner on it, whether a behaviour can be *elicited*, whether
it can be *placed* relative to scene geometry, and whether the resulting reference is *tracked*
by a released tracker (SONIC). The answers for the step-over are "yes", "no", and "no", for one
measurable reason: the text prompt "steps over an obstacle" makes this prior lift the leading
foot inside a **bilateral no-support phase that is not physically ballistic** — in the 12 clips
that exactly clear the staged 5 cm box, both feet leave the calibrated support envelope for
0.44–1.04 s (nine clips) or 2.4–3.1 s (three clips) while the pelvis rises only 0.02–0.26 m,
1.3–16× longer than a ballistic flight of that rise. The elicited step is a float, not a
supported step and not a hop. Its first clearable lift lands early; no spatial channel
moves it, and a prompt delivered late through the released minimum-history streaming interface
elicits it less often (3/16 pooled over EXP-023/023b vs 6/8 from frame 0), 0.8–3 s after the
switch, and never at the predicted place (EXP-023b withdrew "tied to the rollout origin"; 30/49
exp021 clips contain more than one lift region and under windowed re-prompting the tallest
region can come late, so "early" is stated for the tallest region in exp021 and the first
clearable lift in exp023); none of the 44 lifting clips satisfies the pipeline's calibrated 0.2 s contact gate;
and SONIC's evaluation terminations cut every clearing rollout off at or just before the
reference's first no-support run (within 0.2 s in 11/12; within 0.2 s in 47/53 of all terminated
rollouts) with the robot still upright. The calibrated gate — frozen before any of this data
existed — flags 53/53 terminated and passes 8/11 surviving clips, and the same feature transfers
across families (AUC 0.90–0.97). That is what the paper ships: the audit, the gate as a
launch-budget filter that any frozen-prior→tracker pipeline can apply before tracking, the exact
obstacle-centred cost curves, and the tiered data with signed spatial and contact labels — not a
controller.

### 1.3 What the decisive campaigns changed
EXP-022A turned "kinematically placeable in 1 of 5 clips" into "0 retained under the release
evaluator", and EXP-023 turned "the event is early" into "the event is tied to the rollout
origin" (pending its positive control). The contract analysis replaced a story about
*placement* with a story about *what the prompt elicits*: a non-ballistic float whose timing the
interface does not own and whose contact pattern the tracker's evaluator does not follow. Every
earlier negative (exp015/015b "places randomly", exp017/018/019 placement levers, exp020 packet
harm, EXP-022A retention, EXP-023 handoff) is consistent with that one measured property.

**Working title:** *What a Text Prompt Elicits From a Frozen Humanoid Prior, Where It Lands, and
Whether a Tracker Can Follow It.* Alternate: *Before You Plan With a Frozen Motion Prior:
Addressability and Trackability of ARDY-G1 for Obstacle Traversal.* ("Not Tracked" phrasing is
allowed only if EXP-028 shows a physical failure with terminations disabled.)

**Thesis sentence.** A released frozen humanoid motion prior can be asked for a behaviour but not
for its time or place, and what it returns need not be executable; three inexpensive
measurements — elicitation, exact scene-relative placement, and a calibrated contact gate that
predicts tracker cutoff — decide whether the prior is a usable traversal substrate, and for
ARDY-G1 with SONIC they decide it is not, yet.

**Abstract (draft, ~180 words).** Frozen humanoid motion priors are attractive substrates for
scene traversal: natural whole-body motion from data no lab will re-collect, plus a tracker to
execute it. We ask whether such a prior can be *addressed* — asked for a behaviour at a place
and time — and whether what it returns can be *tracked*. On the released ARDY-G1 prior with a
Unitree G1, a text prompt elicits a whole-body-clearable lift in 44/64 clips (69 %, CI 57–79 %),
but every clip that clears the obstacle executes the lift inside a bilateral no-support phase
0.4–3.1 s long, never as a supported step; the event lands early at a rollout-determined time,
and sparse joint rotations, route timing and phase-aligned motion packets fail to move it, while
a prompt delivered late through the released minimum-history streaming interface elicits it less
often (3/16 vs 6/8 from the start), later, and never at the predicted place. Scored with exact obstacle-centred whole-body
clearance at a box centre chosen post hoc, 12/64 clips clear a 5 cm box; under SONIC's release
evaluation terminations all 12 are cut off before the box, upright. The pipeline's calibrated
0.2 s contact gate, frozen beforehand, flags all 53 terminated clips and passes 8 of 11
survivors. We release the protocol, cost curves, and a provenance-bound dataset of 5,393
generated references, 352 of them with tracked outcomes.

### 2.1 Three contributions

**C1 — An addressability audit for frozen motion priors (protocol + ARDY-G1 result).** Elicitation
rate, exact obstacle-centred whole-body clearance, event-time distribution, per-channel
placement gain / compliance / lag, prompt-handoff test with its positive control,
translation-equivariance test — each with its free nominal arm, fresh-seed discipline and
fail-closed gates. *Evidence today:* complete except the handoff positive control. *Closes
with:* A0 analysers (event-frame from qpos, committed) and EXP-023b (WALK→SQUEEZE switch).

**C2 — A trackability audit and a calibrated reference-quality gate.** Paired reference→achieved
retention with a guarded endpoint (non-terminated, in-corridor passage, finishing beyond, exact
clearance); the finding that the elicited step is a non-ballistic float; the calibrated contact
gate as an out-of-sample separator of evaluator termination on the prompt-elicited family
(AUC 0.997; 0.2 s gate 53/53 and 8/11), with within-arm agreement on the position-channel family
that calibrated it (EXP-1C lift arms, AUC 0.92, 9 survivors) and cross-family logistic transfer
0.92/0.90; the physical meaning of "terminated" established by termination-free rollouts.
*Evidence today:* post hoc on exp021 and exp1c. *Closes with:* **EXP-024** (prospective test of
the 0.2 s gate on 128 fresh clips, predictions committed before SONIC; and the contract ablation:
does any native root contract yield a contact-consistent, clearing, retained step), **EXP-028**
(terminations disabled: fell / stalled / walked through / cleared per clip; physics-seed re-roll
as the step family's own test–retest ceiling), and EXP-024b (does SONIC track a physical jump
with a ≥0.3 s flight, i.e. is "flight ⇒ cutoff" a reference property or a tracker property).

**C3 — Cost curves and a labelled hard-negative set with a launch-budget filter.** Exact-centre
hit rates and kinematic best-of-N budgets, zero retained under the release evaluator, tiered
throughput (generated / scored / accepted / executed, with wall-clock and GPU), and the record
schema (scene, request, response, signed spatial error, contract features, kinematic outcome,
evaluator outcome, physics seed, resolved termination config, refusal reason, provenance).
Downstream use, stated without a learned model: at the preregistered 0.2 s gate the filter would
have skipped every SONIC launch that terminated (53/53) at the cost of 3/11 survivors, and the
post hoc 0.32 s cut 51/53 at no survivor cost; reported leave-one-family-out (0.92/0.90). Release
the SONIC motion pickles (regenerated from archived qpos, hash-named in the EXP-022A receipt), the
achieved-state archives, and a consumer-format spec. Not "a dataset for training".

Optional C1/C2 strengthener: **EXP-025 Kimodo-G1** (offline, non-autoregressive released G1
prior; no encoder needed — ARDY's cached STEP embedding is the Kimodo embedding under the
identical LLM2Vec preset). If its elicited lift is also early and also a float, the gate
generalises across priors; if its event-time distribution is broad, "tied to the rollout
origin" becomes autoregressive-specific. Either outcome is reportable; it becomes a **must** if
the runner recovery lands by Sep 4 noon, because it is the only experiment that answers the
"characterisation of one released model" objection.

### 2.2 Why this framing, and what each community takes away
- *Not a negative paper.* The negatives are consequences of one measured property (the float) and
  one reusable instrument (the calibrated gate as a cutoff predictor); reviewers who read
  "evaluation paper" meet a protocol, a prospectively tested gate, a physical-outcome analysis, a
  dataset and (if EXP-025 lands) a cross-prior test.
- *Humanoid-learning researchers* get a checklist to run on any released prior before building a
  planner on it, the ARDY-G1 numbers, and a one-line answer to "why not fine-tune?": RLPF /
  GenTrack / PhysMoDPO close the gap by training; this audit is what one runs before deciding
  whether to fine-tune, on what, and against which tracker.
- *Synthetic-data builders* get the record schema, tiered accounting, and a calibrated filter:
  label generated references by contact consistency and expected evaluator outcome; keep the
  negatives with signed spatial errors.
- *Tracking / navigation researchers* get 352 step-family references with tracked outcomes (plus
  526 duck references / 859 rollouts), a kinematic predictor of evaluator cutoff, and the
  physical outcome classes from EXP-028 — a reference-quality specification a tracker or a
  generator would have to meet to close the loop.

### 2.3 Related-work positioning (≈28 references; ~½ page)
(i) Controllable priors and trackers: ARDY, Kimodo, MotionBricks, SONIC; CLoSD as the legitimate
diffusion-as-planner claimant — no strawman. (ii) Closing the executability gap by training: RLPF,
PhysMoDPO, GenTrack, 2604.17335, SceneAdapt — we are weight-frozen and per-instance, and we
report what to measure first. (iii) Test-time selection, guidance and gating: Texedo (best-of-N
with a learned SONIC-distilled verifier — the learned analogue of our calibrated gate), BRIC,
SafeFlow, ReactiveBFM, BeyondMimic; diffusion-noise optimisation (Karunratanakul 2024) and PRESTO
forbid "frozen ⇒ uncontrollable". **Physical-plausibility metrics for generated motion —
floating, penetration, skating (PhysDiff; the ARDY and Kimodo papers' foot-skating metrics) —
and their use to filter generated data before tracking (2602.21599) or to repair references
into contact consistency (2606.08059) are established; our contribution is not the metric but
the calibrated, per-reference link between the float metric and termination of a specific
released tracker, transferred across two families.** MotionStreamer defines first-frame latency
for streaming text and ARDY's README characterises prompt-adaptation speed vs history length —
cite both. (iv) Perceptive traversal controllers (HumanoidPF, Perceptive Humanoid Parkour,
Gallant, Hiking in the Wild) — orthogonal; we do not compete on traversal. (v)
Language-conditioned humanoids and data pipelines (TextOp on hardware; LangWBC, Harmon, UniAct,
MaskedMimic; CLAW, MTC): language selects the behaviour and several track it (TextOp; Texedo,
RLPF, GenTrack, PhysMoDPO in the loop); none reports when the behaviour occurs relative to prompt
onset, nor per-reference tracking outcomes against a scene-fixed obstacle. Novelty sentence (to
be verified against the **full text** of Texedo, CLoSD, ReactiveBFM, ARDY, TextOp, MotionStreamer,
SafeFlow and BRIC before printing): "to our knowledge no prior work reports where in a rollout a
prompted behaviour lands relative to prompt onset and scene geometry for a released streaming
prior, tests every native channel and a delayed prompt against exact scene-relative clearance,
or ties a calibrated contact gate to per-reference tracker termination."

## 6. Statistics, definitions and claims hygiene

- **Inference unit.** For the multi-scene duck campaigns (Table 2, exp1b: 36 scenes) the scene is
  the inference unit and headline rates carry cluster-bootstrap intervals over scenes. All
  step-over campaigns (exp021, EXP-022A, EXP-1C, EXP-023/023b, EXP-024, EXP-028) use one route and
  one obstacle position: their rates are Wilson intervals over seeds within that single scene and
  are not generalised beyond it. Paired differences at n=8 are descriptive. Bootstrap intervals
  on every AUC. Planned denominators (missing events are failures). Post-hoc analyses are labelled
  post hoc; the 0.2 s gate is the only preregistered predictor.
- **Definitions.** *No-support:* neither foot inside the calibrated support envelope (foot bottom
  ≤ 4.65 cm and planar speed ≤ 1.18 m/s). *Float:* a no-support run of ≥ 5 frames (≥ 0.2 s at
  25 fps) whose duration exceeds the ballistic flight time of the pelvis rise inside it; the
  onset-alignment statistics use this ≥ 0.2 s run. *Calibrated gate:* flags a reference whose
  longest no-support run is **> 0.20 s** (i.e. ≥ 6 frames = 0.24 s on the 25 fps grid; the
  exp016 rule `max_unsupported_run_s ≤ 0.2 s` passes). *Post hoc optimum:* run **> 0.28 s**, i.e.
  **≥ 0.32 s = 8 frames** (51/53 flagged, 11/11 passed); writing it as "> 0.32 s" is wrong (that
  flags 46/53). All thresholds compare against a 0.04 s-quantised feature, so state the
  inclusive frame count whenever a threshold is quoted. *Terminated:*
  SONIC's evaluation configuration ended the episode on a tracking-error threshold — pelvis height
  error > 0.25 m, pelvis orientation error > 1.0 rad, ankle/wrist height error > 0.25 m, ankle
  position error > 0.2 m (checkpoint config merged under the `tracking/eval` override; five active
  terms; resolved config dumped into every receipt; the firing term is not logged). In EXP-022A no
  rollout ended in a fall. *Executed / retained:* achieved-state replay against the collision
  model with the guarded endpoint; the box is absent from Isaac. "Exact clearance at height h at
  x", "clears a box of height h somewhere", and "clears a 5 cm graded level" are three different
  statements; never collapse them.
- **Counts.** "5,393 ARDY generations under the corrected sampler (3,453 in the proposer×feedback
  matrix, 447 in the corpus pilot, 3 stray cache entries, 288 in the step-over ladder, 1,170

## 7. Limitations the paper must state, and risks

Limitations (verbatim into §6 of the paper): one prior (ARDY-G1 Horizon52), one prompt phrasing,
one route, one box depth, one scene for every step-over rate; physics seed 0 with no test–retest
for the step family until EXP-028; "terminated" is an evaluator cutoff whose firing term is not
logged, and no terminated robot had fallen; the gate's calibration corpus is partly stalled
walking; the obstacle is absent from Isaac; the delayed-prompt result is scoped to the Horizon52
checkpoint at the released minimum-history handoff (longer history, Horizon8, re-issued prompts
and latent-state APIs untested) and, until EXP-023b, cannot exclude handoff attenuation; the
cross-prior claim rests on EXP-025.

Risks: (1) "characterisation of one released model" — mitigated by EXP-025, EXP-026, the
protocol and the gate as deliverables; (2) "Texedo already gates with a verifier; PhysDiff
already measures float" — mitigated by citing both prominently and claiming only the calibrated
per-reference link to a released tracker's cutoff; (3) a competing ARDY characterisation before
Sep 15 — no mitigation beyond speed.
