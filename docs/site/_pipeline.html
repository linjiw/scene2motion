How the prior is driven, and what we measure

ARDY-G1 is an autoregressive diffusion motion prior for the Unitree G1: it rolls a clip out in windows, each conditioned on a text embedding, on sparse kinematic constraints, and on the motion it has already produced. We never update its weights. Everything in this work is a question about what its conditioning interface can be made to do.

What we send in

A prompt, a dense root path along the route, and (in the packet arms) full-body global rotations plus a root-height trace over a five-frame window aligned to a measured gait cycle. Identical channel support across compared arms, asserted before generation.

What comes back

A 200-frame, 25 fps joint trajectory. Nothing about clearance, contact, or success is provided by the prior — every endpoint on this page is measured afterwards against the robot's own MuJoCo collision primitives.

Where the tracker sits

SONIC converts a reference clip into executed whole-body motion. It is staged and callable but deliberately not yet in the endpoint: at the 5–8 cm amplitudes this work lives at, tracking error previously consumed most of the margin, so putting it in before the kinematic story was settled would have confounded both.

Why a frozen prior at all

Retraining buys placement trivially and gives up the reason to use a prior: broad, natural, human-like motion learned from data no robotics lab will re-collect. The question worth answering is what the frozen artefact can be made to do — which is what a practitioner picking it off the shelf actually faces.
