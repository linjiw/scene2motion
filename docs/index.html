Contract Before Control: what a frozen humanoid motion prior can and cannot be asked to traverse

We drive NVIDIA's frozen ARDY-G1 motion prior as a scene-conditioned traversal module for the Unitree G1, score every clip against an exact obstacle with a whole-body collision model, and execute the survivors with the SONIC tracker in Isaac. The result is a measurement paper: a trackability contract that predicts the tracker's cutoff before a rollout is spent, a measured-deficit repair that fixes the deficits it can, and a refusal-annotated data engine aimed at downstream humanoid navigation.

Part 1 · Addressability audit

How raw ARDY does as a traversal module

ARDY-G1 (autoregressive diffusion, 25 fps, Horizon52, text prompt + sparse kinematic constraints) is used without weight updates. We asked each native conditioning channel the same four questions: does it elicit the step, keep contact, clear a 5 cm box at the fixed scene position, and is that clearance retained under SONIC?

44 / 64STEP prompt elicits ≥ 3 cm of lift (fresh seeds, exp021)
12 / 64clear a 5 cm box at the fixed centre x = 1.2 m (11/64 at 8 cm)
0 / 64retained under SONIC's guarded endpoint; 53/64 rollouts cut off by the evaluator

What the audit established

  • The prompt is the only channel that elicits reliably, and it elicits once, early. Root-crossing median frame 35 (1.4 s); 40/49 lifting clips lift inside the first 50 frames.
  • No native channel places the step. Position-channel lifts produce bilateral flight; rotation packets show negative compliance (−0.26 / −0.44), no matching lag in [−40, +80] frames, and suppress the prompt's amplitude; route warping preserves the intended gait in 1/6; predeclared-obstacle waiting 1/8.
  • A later prompt elicits the step less often, later, and unplaced. WALK→STEP at frame 52 through the released minimum-history interface: 3/16 pooled (EXP-023 + 023b) against 6/8 from frame 0; latencies 0.84–3.0 s; none clears a box at the predicted centre.
  • Staging is a capability analysis, not a method. Moving the box to a clip's own lift (±0.25 m) reads 0.31; the exact fixed centre reads 0.19. We report both, labelled.
Part 2 · Trackability contract

Why the tracker refuses the elicited step, and how to know before launching

The STEP prompt makes this prior lift the leading foot inside a bilateral no-support phase that is not ballistic: a float, not a supported step and not a hop. In the 12 clips that exactly clear the 5 cm box, both feet leave the calibrated support envelope for 0.44–1.04 s (nine clips) or 2.4–3.1 s (three) while the pelvis rises only 0.02–0.26 m (ballistic ratio 1.3–15.6×).

Definition. A reference is contact-consistent when no bilateral no-support run exceeds 0.20 s (no-support = neither foot bottom ≤ 4.65 cm with planar speed ≤ 1.18 m/s). The gate is not a lift detector: lift height alone gives AUC 0.92, and the run feature still separates the 20 non-lifting clips (AUC 0.98). Transfer to the position-channel family: 0.92.

Part 3 · Measured-deficit repair

Where measurement can fix the reference: overhead clearance

Ducking under a beam is a scalar-coupled, monotone response: more realised flexion moves overhead clearance in a measurable direction, so the current error identifies a repair. Stepping is a multi-phase contact transition with no tested scalar inverse. The system therefore repairs the first class and converts the second into quantified refusal before control.

Extrapolated rate at the kinematically-verified tier: 7.7 × 10⁴ records per GPU-day from the 302 s pilot, reported with its tier and never as a single "10⁵ verified clips" figure. Every record carries provenance (HF revision, denoiser sha, runtime commit, robot model sha, tracker commit + checkpoint, physics seed, resolved termination config) and, when refused, the reason and the measured deficit.

Why the negatives matter

Imitation corpora keep only successes. A downstream policy that has never seen a quantified refusal (scene geometry, binding constraint, measured deficit 0.28 m, maximum usable envelope 0.88 m) has no signal for when to stop or re-route. Our records keep the accepted motions with their margins and the rejected and refused ones with their deficits, so the corpus teaches both where the body can go and where its physical envelope ends. As a launch-budget filter alone, the 0.20 s gate would have skipped every terminating SONIC launch (53/53) at the cost of 3/11 survivors.

Proposal · beyond the ICRA paper

Posture-conditional traversability for humanoid navigation

planned · not yet evidence

Embodied world generators and navigation stacks treat traversability as binary: inflate a capsule, erode the free space, and any cell with something in its vertical column is blocked. A humanoid does not work that way. A corridor with 1.3 m of clearance is fully traversable at a 0.31 m crouch. We define traversability as a function of the posture the body can adopt, and the engineering question becomes: what posture did the stochastic prior actually produce, and can the physical controller track it?

conventional 2-D / 3-D navigation obstacle anywhere in the vertical column ──► cell blocked: F_i = Erode(W, r_i) posture-conditional navigation (this project) overhead clearance h(s) along the route ──► traversable if the body can deform: F(d) = { s | clearance(s) ≥ standing height − d + margin(d) }

The four-stage production pipeline

[parametric scene grammar: procedural rooms + clutter] │ ▼ [stage 1 A* topological routing over (x, y, body mode)] │ ▼ [stage 2 contract-gated measured-deficit generation] ├── overhead deficit ──► forward-secant repair (≤ 2 iterations) └── support contract ──► gate on r_max ≤ 0.20 s │ ┌────────────┴────────────┐ [admitted] [refused] │ │ ▼ ▼ [stage 3 headless SONIC rollout] [structured refusal record: labelled hard negative] │ ┌────┴────┐ [physically [evaluator cutoff / divergence] tracked] (dynamics mismatch, with its timestamp and firing term)

Stages 1–2 and the record schema exist today (scene2motion/planner.py, scene2motion/verify/, optim/, docs/dataset-engine-record-schema.md); stage 3 exists as per-campaign SONIC bridges. What is new in the proposal is the scene grammar, the tiered difficulty ladder and a single engine that runs all four stages on procedural rooms.

Scene grammar and difficulty tiers

Tier 1 · open passage

Doorways ≥ 2.0 m. Establishes the tracker's dynamic stability on plain walking.

Tier 2 · continuous overhead (duck)

Low lintels, pipes, hanging clutter, h ∈ [0.95, 1.40] m. Exercises Class I scalar repair and depth-dependent tracking.

Tier 3 · discrete ground (step)

Thresholds, cables, low boxes, h ∈ [0.03, 0.15] m. Exercises the support-phase gate and the refusal logic.

Tier 4 · composites

Alternating beams and ground obstacles with branching routes, forcing the body-aware router to trade a deep crouch against an upright detour.

Yield funnel the release will report

generated ──► kinematically collision-free ──► gate-admitted (r_max ≤ 0.20 s) ──► SONIC rollout ──► retained at the obstacle └──► refused / rejected, with reason and measured deficit
Proposal · evaluation under SONIC

Measuring tracking quality and the road to hardware

planned · schema v3 export required for torque channels

Subtask progress, not only success rate

Report the number of obstacles cleared before the first collision or cutoff, and the per-obstacle failure hazard h(k). A flat h(k) means obstacles are independent and repair cost scales linearly; a rising h(k) means momentum couples consecutive obstacles.

Execution-aware margin τ(d)

Fit required margin = cbase + κ₀ + κ₁·d from torso and wrist tracking residuals, then test whether references carrying τ(d) pass the physics scene collision-free. Our earlier attempt had zero uncensored observations; this needs an interaction-reaching campaign.

Contact-force consistency

Temporal overlap between simulated normal contact force and the kinematic support mask Sj(t). Floats should show the mismatch directly.

Base drift and torque saturation margin

Accumulated linear/angular deviation of the floating base from the reference pelvis frame, and the fraction of the cycle with |τi| ≤ 0.8 τmax. The current archive stores qpos only; exporting qvel and applied torque is the schema v3 change.

Downstream consumers

The records are shaped for three consumers: a student whole-body navigation policy trained by privileged-to-proprioceptive distillation, an RL policy that receives the refusals as a shaped penalty with the quantified deficit, and a vision-language-action model that needs a per-scene label of which posture made a route traversable. The standard test is held-out procedural rooms, traversed under SONIC, scored by retained obstacle clearance and subtask progress. A downstream training result is not claimed until it exists.

Work packages that need no GPU

  • Dataset export engine (scene2motion/dataset_engine.py): the tiered record schema, a manifest summariser that turns the 300-scene pilot into the funnel table, and an export that SONIC's rollout bridge reads directly.
  • Subtask hazard analysis (experiments/analyze_subtask_hazard.py) and the tracking-quality channels in the SONIC wrapper, with figure scripts for h(k) and tracking error versus crouch depth.
  • Downstream integration memo (docs/downstream-navigation-benchmark.md): how policies consume the records, a joint positive/negative loss, and the held-out room protocol.
