# Contract Before Control: Gating and Repairing Frozen Humanoid Motion Priors for Scene-Relative Traversal

**Anonymous Authors**

**ICRA 2027 Submission Draft**

## Abstract

Frozen generative motion priors offer a high-bandwidth interface to humanoid control, but their conditioning variables are soft requests: a motion can look semantically correct while occurring at the wrong scene location, or clear an obstacle through a reference that a tracker should never be asked to realize. We factor scene-relative capability into behavior elicitation $E_b$, exact obstacle-relative placement $P_o$, an interpretable reference support contract $C$, and guarded execution retention $R_o$. We then introduce **Contract-Gated Measured-Deficit Synthesis**, a weight-frozen pipeline that measures realized motion, directionally repairs monotone overhead-clearance deficits, and refuses references that violate the support contract. On 36 out-of-distribution multi-beam scenes, two repairs raise a coarse TCN proposal's collision-free rate from $0.729$ to $0.993$, compared with $0.774$ for equal-budget three-sample selection. Conversely, a prompted step-over family elicits at least $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ of lift in $44/64$ references but places only $12/64$ above a $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ obstacle at the required center; all 12 clear through non-ballistic bilateral float. A calibrated $0.20\,\mathrm{s}$ no-support gate predicts the released evaluator's cutoff label with ROC AUC $0.997$ retrospectively. The evidence supports a scoped claim: measurement can repair failures with a monotone geometric direction, while apparent clearance that violates the contract should produce quantified refusal before control. The resulting records include accepted motions, margins, deficits, and hard negatives for downstream learning; they do not establish hardware obstacle traversal.

**Index Terms—** humanoid motion generation, scene-conditioned motion, motion tracking, test-time repair, runtime gating, negative-data generation.

## I. Introduction

Generative human and humanoid motion models increasingly expose a tempting systems abstraction: treat a pretrained, frozen prior as a scene actuator. Text requests the behavior, a route places it in an environment, sparse constraints steer individual joints, and a whole-body controller attempts to reproduce the resulting reference. This interface could amortize motion design across many obstacles without retraining the generator for every scene. It also hides a consequential mismatch. Conditioning variables in a learned prior are soft statistical requests, not commands with geometric or physical semantics. A motion may contain the right visual motif yet occur a meter after the obstacle; a foot may rise above a box while both feet hover and the pelvis remains nearly level. Sending either reference to a strong tracker does not repair the upstream ambiguity—it makes the tracker the first component asked to discover it.

The practical pain is not merely a lower aggregate success rate. When generation, placement, physical plausibility, and tracking are collapsed into one binary outcome, a researcher cannot tell whether to improve a prompt, alter a constraint, resample a stochastic model, repair a geometric deficit, or train a new controller. This ambiguity wastes expensive rollouts and contaminates datasets: visually convincing but non-trackable references can be labeled as successful obstacle interaction, while informative refusals disappear from corpora designed only around positive demonstrations. Reproduction is also weakened because a single reported rate silently combines generator seeds, scene instances, tracker behavior, and evaluator termination rules.

We instead factor the use of a frozen prior into four separately testable events. For a requested behavior $b$, obstacle $o$, generated reference $x_{\mathrm{ref}}$, and executed trajectory $x_{\mathrm{exec}}$, we ask

\[
E_b:\text{Was the behavior elicited?},\qquad
P_o:\text{Was it placed at the obstacle?},
\]
\[
C:\text{Does the reference satisfy a support contract?},\qquad
R_o:\text{Was obstacle-relative clearance retained under execution?}
\]

The intended chain is $E_b\rightarrow P_o\rightarrow C\rightarrow R_o$; evidence for an earlier event is not evidence for a later one. In particular, $E_b\wedge P_o$ is only a kinematic proposal, and $C$ is a calibrated admission rule rather than a general physical guarantee. This decomposition changes the system's action at failure. A positive, local overhead deficit admits a directional repair because more joint flexion produces a measurable monotone clearance response. A prolonged no-support trace has no analogous scene-local correction: the system should quantify the violation and refuse the proposal before control.

Fig. 1 summarizes this bifurcation. A frozen generator is first treated as an instrument whose realized traces must be measured, not as an oracle whose conditioning inputs are assumed to have taken effect. The same measurement layer can then either close a short geometric loop or stop the pipeline at a stated contract boundary. This organization makes “no valid proposal found” a legitimate terminal output rather than an implementation failure.

This paper makes three contributions:

1. **A contract-gated formulation for frozen humanoid priors.** We separate elicitation, exact placement, reference admission, and executed retention, and define an interpretable support contract calibrated against a released tracker/evaluator pair.
2. **Measured-deficit repair with explicit refusal.** We present a test-time procedure that applies a forward-secant update to monotone overhead deficits, enforces a two-repair budget, and refuses lateral/support failures for which no measured repair mode is available.
3. **An execution-audited empirical artifact.** Across multi-beam ducking and exact-center step-over studies, we report scene-level and seed-level units separately, preserve prospective from post-hoc endpoints, and retain deficits, cutoffs, and refusals as training data rather than discarding them.

Our central claim is deliberately narrower than end-to-end autonomy: **before a frozen motion prior is used as a scene actuator, its proposal should be measured against a task-specific reference contract; measurement can repair monotone geometric deficits, while contract violations should produce quantified refusal before control.** The experiments establish the utility of this separation for the studied Unitree G1 pipeline. They do not claim that passing the contract proves physical safety or that the current system completes physical obstacle traversal.

## II. Related Work

**Generative motion priors.** Text-conditioned diffusion and autoregressive models have expanded the range and duration of synthesized motion. ARDY [1], used here without weight updates, combines language with sparse kinematic control for long-horizon humanoid motion. Kimodo [2], MotionStreamer [3], and MotionBricks [4] similarly demonstrate increasingly compositional or streaming motion generation. These works motivate a reusable motion interface, but semantic quality and constraint response do not by themselves certify obstacle-relative placement or trackability. Our concern is the systems boundary around such a prior: we measure what its controls realized in scene coordinates and decide whether to repair, admit, or refuse the sample.

**Diffusion planning and physics tracking.** Physics-aware generation and tracking methods can improve dynamic consistency either during synthesis or during execution. CLoSD [5] couples language-conditioned diffusion with closed-loop control; PhysDiff [6] introduces a physics-based motion projection mechanism; SONIC [7] tracks diverse humanoid references with a learned whole-body controller. Our pipeline is complementary. It assumes both generator and tracker are frozen, and places a cheap, interpretable admission layer between them. This matters because a capable tracker is not obliged to realize a reference that encodes prolonged unsupported translation, and its evaluator cutoff cannot retrospectively make the original scene claim well-posed.

**Test-time selection, guidance, and gating.** Recent systems already go well beyond unfiltered generation. TEXEDO [10] samples candidates and selects with controller-aware dynamic and semantic verifiers; SafeFlow [8] combines physics-guided generation with staged deployment gating; BRIC [9] adapts a physics controller and guides a frozen planner in signal space at test time. A frozen generator followed by a filter is therefore not our novelty claim. Our narrower wedge is exact-center scene evaluation, directional repair derived from the measured clearance response, and a low-dimensional support contract whose violation is named. We compare repair directly with equal-budget stochastic resampling so that the value of measurement is not conflated with extra generator calls.

**Perceptive whole-body control.** HumanoidPF [11] learns an obstacle-relative potential-field representation for crouching, hurdling, and narrow-passage traversal, while Moving Through Clutter [12] couples embodiment-consistent motion with 145 cluttered scenes. These systems establish a broader scene-aware control target than our procedural corridor. Our question is different: can a general-purpose frozen motion prior reliably supply references to an existing tracker without retraining either component? Refused floats, exact-placement misses, and execution cutoffs are useful hard negatives for training a future perceptive policy to distinguish a human-readable motif from a dynamically grounded obstacle action.

## III. Problem Formulation and the Trackability Contract

### A. A Frozen Prior as a Stochastic Actuator

Let $u$ be a natural-language request, $z$ a random seed, $h$ a set of sparse kinematic controls, and $o$ a scene obstacle expressed in route coordinates. A frozen motion prior produces

\[
x_{\mathrm{ref}} = G_\theta(u,z,h), \qquad \theta\ \text{fixed},
\tag{1}
\]

and a frozen tracker maps the reference and execution disturbance $\xi$ to

\[
x_{\mathrm{exec}} = T_\phi(x_{\mathrm{ref}},\xi), \qquad \phi\ \text{fixed}.
\tag{2}
\]

The reference contains root and joint trajectories at $25\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The scene evaluator derives body and collision-foot traces by forward kinematics. For an obstacle with route center $s_o$, let $c_{\mathrm{over}}(s)$ be signed overhead clearance over the occupied route interval, and $c_{\mathrm{lat}}(s_o)$ be obstacle-relative foot clearance at the exact center. Positive values denote separation under the simulation collision primitives described below. The placement event $P_o$ is evaluated at $s_o$, not at the largest lift found anywhere in the clip.

This distinction prevents a common metric substitution. Define $L_{\max}$ as the largest clearable foot lift anywhere along a reference. Then $L_{\max}\ge h_o$ establishes elicitation for an obstacle height $h_o$, but it does not establish $c_{\mathrm{lat}}(s_o)>0$. A motion can satisfy the first inequality while missing the obstacle in time or route position.

### B. Scene Geometry and Coverage Margin

The evaluation scene uses MuJoCo primitives fitted to the Unitree G1 body and feet. All clearance tests include a measured $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ coverage margin around these simulation collision primitives. We therefore report collision status with respect to this conservative model, not a geometrically exact body model. Ducking scenes contain multiple overhead beams; step scenes contain a box centered at $x=1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ on a $7.2\,\mathrm{m}$ straight route. The reference and executed trajectories are transformed into the same route frame before clearance is measured.

### C. Reference Support Contract

Kinematic clearance alone can be achieved by an implausible float. We define a support indicator from foot-bottom height and planar foot speed. Foot $j\in\{L,R\}$ is in reference support at frame $t$ when

\[
S_j(t)=\mathbb{1}\!\left[z^{\mathrm{bottom}}_j(t)\le 0.0465\,\mathrm{m}
\;\land\;
\|v^{xy}_j(t)\|\le1.18\,\mathrm{m/s}\right].
\tag{3}
\]

Let $N(t)=1-[S_L(t)\lor S_R(t)]$, and let $r_{\max}(x_{\mathrm{ref}})$ be the duration of the longest contiguous run for which $N(t)=1$. The primary, prospectively fixed gate is

\[
C(x_{\mathrm{ref}})=
\mathbb{1}\!\left[r_{\max}(x_{\mathrm{ref}})\le0.20\,\mathrm{s}\right].
\tag{4}
\]

At $25\,\mathrm{Hz}$, a violation $r_{\max}>0.20\,\mathrm{s}$ begins with a run of at least six frames. The height and speed thresholds were calibrated from ordinary support traces; they are not estimated anew for each obstacle or seed.

We also compute a diagnostic ballistic bound. If the pelvis rises by $\Delta z>0$, an idealized ballistic excursion under gravity has duration scale

\[
T_{\mathrm{ball}}(\Delta z)=2\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta z}{g}}.
\tag{5}
\]

The ratio $r_{\max}/T_{\mathrm{ball}}$ is not an admission threshold, but it exposes unsupported intervals that cannot be explained by the observed vertical pelvis motion. A flat pelvis with long bilateral support loss is thus distinct from a jump even if both feet clear the obstacle.

### D. Capability Events and Admissible Claims

For each proposal we record four events:

\[
E_b=\mathbb{1}[\text{requested motif occurs}],\quad
P_o=\mathbb{1}[c_o(x_{\mathrm{ref}})\ge0],
\tag{6}
\]
\[
C\ \text{from (4)},\quad
R_o=\mathbb{1}[c_o(x_{\mathrm{exec}})\ge0\land\text{route endpoint reached}].
\tag{7}
\]

The definition of $c_o$ depends on the obstacle family: overhead body clearance for ducking and foot/box separation at the fixed center for stepping. The pipeline admits a proposal to the tracker only after the reference-side task conditions and $C$ pass. $R_o$ is then an execution result, not an inferred property of $x_{\mathrm{ref}}$. An evaluator cutoff is recorded as such; it is not relabeled as a physical outcome without a separately defined endpoint.

## IV. Contract-Gated Measured-Deficit Synthesis

### A. Architecture

The framework has five operations: **propose, trace, gate, repair, and route**. The prior first samples a proposal using the requested text, route, and optional sparse controls. Forward kinematics produces obstacle-relative body, foot, and support traces, and the support gate is applied to every proposal. If the contract passes, the measured response class determines the next operation. A Class I overhead trace exposes a signed clearance deficit and a scalar repair direction. A Class II lateral trace must additionally satisfy exact-center placement; its placement miss or support violation is not converted into an overhead-style update because the current interface exposes no validated monotone control over contact phase and event location. The output is an admitted reference, a bounded failure after repair, or a quantified refusal.

### B. Forward-Secant Overhead Repair

Let $q(s)$ denote the scalar crouch command along route progress $s$, $m$ the desired clearance margin, and $c_{\mathrm{over}}(s;q)$ the measured overhead clearance. The nonnegative deficit is

\[
e(s)=\max\{0,\,m-c_{\mathrm{over}}(s;q)\}.
\tag{8}
\]

The realized response of the frozen generator need not equal the requested command. We estimate a forward secant $g'_+(q)$ from a small positive probe and update

\[
\Delta q(s)=\frac{e(s)}{\max(|g'_+(q(s))|,\,0.15)}.
\tag{9}
\]

The secant substitutes measured generator response for command-space faith. This is important near centered beams, where a nominal inverse can undershoot the required correction by up to $27\%$. The repair mask begins $3\tau v$ ahead of the measured deficit, where $\tau$ is response delay and $v$ is route speed. We smooth and clip the update, preserve monotonicity toward greater clearance, and permit at most two repaired generations. Every generation is remeasured; no update receives credit from its requested amplitude alone.

### C. Algorithm and State Transitions

Algorithm 1 exposes proposal, behavior detection, support tracing, placement tracing, and overhead tracing as separate typed routines. `DETECT_BEHAVIOR` uses the fixed family-specific predicates in Sec. V rather than visual judgment; every other branch operates on signed geometric quantities.

**Algorithm 1 — Contract-Gated Measured-Deficit Synthesis**

~~~text
INPUT  G_theta, u, o, h_0, advancing seeds {z_k}, margin m,
       response lag tau, route speed v, repair budget B = 2,
       support cutoff r_star = 0.20 s
OUTPUT (state, x, record)
DEFINE RET(s,r,delta,D) := (s, x, {reason:r, delta_deficit:delta, traces:D})

ROUTINES
  PROPOSE(G_theta,u,z,h) -> x;  DETECT_BEHAVIOR(x,u) -> E
  TRACE_SUPPORT(x) -> S=(r_max,pelvis_rise,ballistic_ratio)
  TRACE_PLACEMENT(x,o) -> P=(exact,clearance,center_offset)
  TRACE_OVERHEAD(x,o,m) -> D=(c_min,deficit_curve e)
  REPAIR(h,D) -> Smooth(MonotoneClip(h + Delta_q(D.e), shift=3*tau*v))

h <- h_0
FOR k = 0,...,B:
  x <- PROPOSE(G_theta,u,z_k,h);  E <- DETECT_BEHAVIOR(x,u)
  IF not E.present: RETURN RET(refused, NO_ELICITED_MODE, E.deficit, E)

  S <- TRACE_SUPPORT(x)
  IF S.r_max > r_star:
    RETURN RET(refused, SUPPORT_CONTRACT, S.r_max-r_star, (E,S))

  IF kind(o) = LATERAL:
    P <- TRACE_PLACEMENT(x,o)
    IF not P.exact:
      RETURN RET(refused, EXACT_PLACEMENT,
                 (P.center_offset, max(0,-P.clearance)), (E,S,P))
    IF P.clearance >= m: RETURN RET(accepted, NONE, 0, (E,S,P))
    RETURN RET(accepted_margin, MARGIN, m-P.clearance, (E,S,P))

  D <- TRACE_OVERHEAD(x,o,m)
  IF D.c_min >= m: RETURN RET(accepted, NONE, 0, (E,S,D))
  IF k = B AND D.c_min >= 0:
    RETURN RET(accepted_margin, MARGIN, m-D.c_min, (E,S,D))
  IF k = B:
    RETURN RET(rejected, REPAIR_BUDGET, -D.c_min, (E,S,D))
  h <- REPAIR(h,D)  # Delta_q follows Eqs. (8)--(9)
~~~

Every branch returns the same typed tuple $(\text{state},x_{\mathrm{ref}},d)$; $d$ always contains the reason, signed residual when nonzero, and measurement traces needed to reproduce the decision. The transition logic is asymmetric by design. `rejected` means that a validated repair operator exhausted its budget while collision remained. `refused` means that no validated repair applies or the support contract fails. `accepted_margin` is collision-free but retains a nonzero requested-margin deficit. Only `accepted` and policy-approved `accepted_margin` records are eligible for $T_\phi$; $R_o$ is appended as a separate execution result.

### D. Selection, Refusal, and Auditability

Repair and resampling answer different questions. Resampling searches the prior's stochastic support and is appropriate when many valid modes exist and only extra margin is desired. Repair uses a signed measurement to change the next proposal and is appropriate when the failure direction is locally monotone. We evaluate them at matched generation budgets. The seed stream advances on every call, so a repaired sample is not falsely described as a deterministic edit of its predecessor. Each record stores the original proposal, measured traces, requested update, realized change, contract decision, tracker disposition, and terminal state.

Refusal includes a named reason and magnitude: absent motif, exact-center displacement, unsupported-run excess, or unavailable repair channel. This makes the framework operational without implying that every task is repairable. In the present implementation, overhead deficits have a validated repair direction; lateral timing and support violations do not.

## V. Experimental Setup

### A. Models, Robot, and Scenes

We use the frozen ARDY-G1 Horizon52 prior [1] at $25\,\mathrm{Hz}$, five DDIM steps, and an advancing random stream. Inputs are text, a straight root route, and, where indicated, sparse joint-position packets. References are converted to Unitree G1 joint trajectories. Execution studies use the released SONIC tracker [7]. Reference collision measurements use MuJoCo G1 primitives with the $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ coverage margin; the tracker environment does not contain the obstacle, so execution tests measure retention of an obstacle-relative reference, not reaction to obstacle contact.

The overhead benchmark contains 36 procedural multi-beam scenes with three to six beams, outside the proposal models' training distribution. Eight seeds are evaluated per scene. The lateral benchmark uses a $7.2\,\mathrm{m}$ straight route with a box centered at $x=1.2\,\mathrm{m}$, at heights $3$, $5$, and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$. Prompt and sparse-control studies isolate elicitation from exact placement before any tracker call.

### B. Baselines and Endpoints

For ducking, three proposal families—a heuristic, a quadratic-program (QP) proposal, and a temporal convolutional network (TCN)—are evaluated alone, after one or two measured repairs, and with best-of-two or best-of-three independent resampling. The main endpoints are collision-free rate, target-margin rate for $m=0.18\,\mathrm{m}$, and mean generator calls. For stepping, the reference endpoints are any-location lift, exact-center clearance, longest no-support run, pelvis rise, and ballistic ratio. The execution endpoint is the released evaluator's cutoff/continuation label plus exact obstacle-relative retention.

The primary support threshold $0.20\,\mathrm{s}$ is fixed before the fresh prospective reference batch. The retrospective ROC uses the earlier 64-reference set. A separately reported $0.28\,\mathrm{s}$ rule is a post-hoc optimum: because the predicate is $r_{\max}>0.28\,\mathrm{s}$, its first flagged duration at $25\,\mathrm{Hz}$ is $0.32\,\mathrm{s}$. We do not present it as prospectively calibrated.

### C. Statistical Units and Reproducibility

Scene is the inference unit for the 36-scene ducking benchmark; eight generator seeds are replicates within a scene. Seed is the unit for step-family proportions. Binomial intervals are reported where they clarify finite-sample uncertainty, and bootstrap intervals are used for ROC AUC. Post-hoc thresholds, obstacle locations selected after viewing traces, and estimated sample budgets are labeled explicitly. Every result row is tied to a machine-readable receipt; tracker results are inserted only after the corresponding receipt lands. This rule yields fixed macros for unfinished execution stages without interrupting the paper's argument.

### B. Class II: Rejecting the Multi-Phase Kinematic Mirage

Class II behavior couples lift, swing timing, support exchange, root dynamics, and scene location. A height-only metric therefore overstates capability: a start-of-clip “step over” prompt elicits at least $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ of whole-body-clearable lift somewhere in 44/64 references ($0.69$, approximately $[0.57,0.79]$), but exact-center clearance at $x=1.2\,\mathrm{m}$ is only 13/64, 12/64, and 11/64 for $3$, $5$, and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ boxes. The midroute control at $x=3.6\,\mathrm{m}$ yields 2/64. Thus the prior contains a lift motif, $E_b$, without exposing reliable obstacle placement, $P_o$.

The red region in Fig. 2 reveals the more decisive failure. All 12 exact-center $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ references clear through bilateral support loss lasting $0.44$–$3.12\,\mathrm{s}$ while the pelvis rises only $0.02$–$0.26\,\mathrm{m}$. Their unsupported duration is $1.3$–$15.6\times$ the ballistic scale implied by that rise. The nearly level pelvis and translating root distinguish the motion from a jump; the reference satisfies a box-height predicate by floating.

Native sparse packets do not supply the missing phase-and-location inverse. In the selected absolute and residual arms, they elicit lift in 8/8 references but place it at the exact center in 0/8; residual events remain $1.32$–$1.59\,\mathrm{m}$ from the target, while absolute events occur at median route position $2.33\,\mathrm{m}$. In a separate ablation, adding either packet reduces mean lift from the text-only $0.121\,\mathrm{m}$ to $0.072$ or $0.061\,\mathrm{m}$ and changes 3/8 text-only exact clearances to 0/8. Larger amplitudes also fail: across 288 direct-position references, realized lift overshoots the request by $1.68$–$2.32\times$, yet $91\%$–$98\%$ of the interval is bilateral flight, and tracker completion falls from $0.25$ at $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ to zero at $16\,\mathrm{cm}$ and above. These observations motivate refusal for Class II failures rather than an unvalidated scalar repair.

The separately styled post-hoc point, $r_{\max}>0.28\,\mathrm{s}$, flags 51/53 and passes 11/11; it is not presented as prospective calibration.

The right panel establishes temporal alignment without reinterpreting cutoff as impact. In 47/53 cutoff executions, termination occurs within $0.20\,\mathrm{s}$ of support-loss onset; the median offset is $+0.04\,\mathrm{s}$. All last archived postures have pelvis height $0.56$–$0.95\,\mathrm{m}$, none is below $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$, and only one exceeds $23^\circ$ tilt. The gate should therefore be read as an **operational input contract for this tracker/evaluator pair**: it withholds references whose support timing differs sharply from ordinary calibration traces and is associated with evaluator cutoff. It neither proves membership outside SONIC's training distribution nor predicts the later physical outcome. Termination-disabled rollouts in **[EXP-028-OUTCOME-SLOT]** are the required test of whether these references subsequently diverge, stall, recover, or retain obstacle-relative clearance.

### D. Native Channels and Prompt-Latency Bounds

To test whether the Class II failure is merely a prompt-timing error, we compare from-start and prefix-preserving delayed prompts. “Step over” from the initial window elicits an event in 6/8 seeds. A frame-52 handoff elicits 3/16 across two batches, and a frame-104 handoff elicits 0/8 in its tested batch. The three delayed events arrive $0.84$–$3.0\,\mathrm{s}$ after the switch, and none of the 16 pooled frame-52 references clears the fixed obstacle center at any tested height. Post-switch joint RMS changes of $0.14$–$0.20\,\mathrm{rad}$ verify that the continuation changed despite its exact shared prefix; the negative result is not a no-op decoder call.

The bounded conclusion is that later prompting elicits the behavior less often, later, and without exact placement—not that the mode is irreversibly tied to route origin. Sparse packets likewise suppress rather than translate the event. An intended substrate control, “squeeze,” elicits no lateral sidestep in 0/8 references and instead produces lift in 5/8. Algorithm 1 therefore returns `refused: NO_ELICITED_MODE` rather than inventing a repair direction.

Together, Sections VI-B–D locate the current bottleneck. The prior possesses a step-like semantic mode, but neither text scheduling nor the tested native kinematic channels reliably place that mode, and the exact-center subset violates the support contract. The appropriate near-term output is a precise no-go result for the tested interface—not a claim that a different generator, learned placement controller, or perceptive policy could not solve the task.

## VII. Data Engine and Downstream Impact

Contract-gated synthesis produces more than a filtered success set. Each generation yields a tiered record: conditioning and seed; route-frame kinematics; collision and support traces; signed deficits and repair response; terminal state; and, when executed, tracker/evaluator outcomes. The audited execution corpus currently contains 878 unique references with tracker outcomes—526 duck and 352 step-family references—while analyses preserve campaign-specific denominators instead of pooling them. A 300-scene overhead run takes $301.9\,\mathrm{s}$ on an RTX 5080, or $0.994$ evaluated scenes/s, and yields 192 `accepted`, 76 `accepted_margin`, 6 `rejected`, and 26 `refused` records. Raw throughput projects to $8.6\times10^4$ evaluated scenes/day; multiplying by the observed $268/300$ kinematically verified yield gives the reported $7.7\times10^4$ verified records/day. Both are reference-side projections, not executed-rollout counts.

Negative records are first-class data. A residual overhead collision gives a localized clearance deficit; a placement miss gives a signed route displacement; a float gives support-loss duration, pelvis rise, and ballistic ratio; a tracker cutoff preserves its onset alignment and last archived posture. These labels can supervise a learned admission model, initialize a perceptive whole-body policy, or support privileged-to-observable distillation. They can also prevent a downstream learner from treating a visually salient lift as a valid step merely because a foot crossed a height threshold. The broader value of the framework is thus not only fewer wasted rollouts, but a dataset whose negatives explain which link of $E_b\rightarrow P_o\rightarrow C\rightarrow R_o$ failed.

## VIII. Limitations and Conclusion

The evidence is restricted to one robot morphology, one frozen prior, one released tracker, procedural corridors, and simulation collision primitives with a $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ margin. The Class I/Class II taxonomy is structural, but its present empirical support is ARDY-specific. A protocol-drafted, not-yet-preregistered cross-prior audit proposes 64 STEP and 64 WALK references from the offline Kimodo-G1 prior; **[EXP-025-KIMODO-CROSS-PRIOR-SLOT]** will test whether event timing and support violations transfer. Until that receipt lands, the comparator remains the ARDY-only 44/64 elicitation and 12/12 exact-center float result. The obstacle is also absent from the tracker environment, so the execution audit measures reference retention rather than collision response.

Duck execution remains unresolved: 859 rollouts over 526 unique references contain no complete $7.2\,\mathrm{m}$ traversal, but a $14\,\mathrm{s}$ clip cap forces $1.04$–$1.79\,\mathrm{m/s}$ travel and even zero-dip controls stall near $2.15\,\mathrm{m}$. Those runs are insufficient to identify duck trackability or estimate a clearance-dependent retention function. Seed-level reroll disagreement further cautions against counting repeated rollouts as independent references.

Three closures remain deliberately isolated from the main claim. The prospective 128-reference tracker study will populate **[EXP-024-AUC-LANDED]**, **[EXP-024-2X2-LANDED]**, and **[EXP-024-RETENTION-LANDED]** without changing its frozen Table II baseline. A matched physical-jump trace will populate **[EXP-024B-JUMP-CONTROL-SLOT]** for Fig. 2 beside the current $0.02$–$0.26\,\mathrm{m}$ pelvis-rise and $1.3$–$15.6\times$ float baselines. A separate execution study will report predefined physical-outcome categories in **[EXP-028-OUTCOME-SLOT]** and seed-level agreement in **[EXP-028-SEED-AGREEMENT-SLOT]**; its immutable evaluator baseline is 53/64 cutoffs, 47/53 within $0.20\,\mathrm{s}$ of support-loss onset, and median offset $+0.04\,\mathrm{s}$. Until that receipt exists, evaluator cutoff and physical outcome remain distinct. Future work should also learn a temporally grounded placement interface, extend contracts to hand and multi-contact support, add obstacle contact to execution, validate on hardware, and test whether contract negatives improve downstream reinforcement learning.

Within these boundaries, the experiments support one consistent conclusion. A frozen motion prior should not be promoted from generator to scene actuator on semantic appearance alone. Exact tracing reveals whether a behavior occurred where needed; measured forward-secants repair monotone overhead deficits far more efficiently than blind resampling when proposals are poor; and a simple calibrated support contract detects the non-ballistic float hidden by nominal step clearance. **Contract before control** is therefore both a method and an accounting rule: repair what has a measured direction, refuse what violates the stated contract, and reserve execution claims for references that pass both.
