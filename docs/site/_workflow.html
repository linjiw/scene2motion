Process

How the work actually went

Nine of the fifteen campaigns behind this page stopped without producing the result they were built to produce. That is the method working, not the method failing: each was preregistered with gates that could refuse it, and every refusal is kept as evidence and diagnosed before anything is changed.

What stopped itWhereWhat the refusal taught
Substrate attritioncalibration v1 Two seeds had no complete gait cycle at any speed — ~6 % of seeds are washouts, which every later pool budget had to carry.
Optimistic gatecalibration v2 A coverage rate estimated at 5/7 measured 3/7. The gate was resized to the measurement, on fresh seeds, and passed at v3.
Pool exhaustionexp017 Waiting for a gait event at a predeclared obstacle yields 1 seed in 8. The fixed-frame design was closed rather than widened.
Gait re-planningexp018 Warping route timing to move the root re-plans the gait: 1 of 6 seeds kept the swing the program was built around.
Two enumeratorsexp019 v1 Placement was chosen with one cycle detector and executed with another. Fixed by making selection dry-run the real render — outcome-free, but binding.
Insufficient controlexp019 v3 All arms scored zero and we first read it as a broken scene. The free nominal arm proved the endpoint was simply strict: a walk clears nothing anywhere.
Our own estimateexp019 v5–v6 An offline replay predicted 13/32 eligibility; three real pools returned 6, 6 and 5. The estimate was retracted in the record, and the pool doubled.
Two defects the process caught that a passing run would have hidden exp017's channel guard listed names from the wrong namespace, so it would have aborted any real arm generation — it survived only because no campaign had yet reached that line. And the first complete arm set was scored on scenes we had mis-specified; the control arm that cost nothing to add is what made that legible. Both are in the committed record with the commits that fixed them.
