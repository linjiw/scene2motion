What the pipeline emits, and why it is a dataset

A frozen prior plus a geometric scorer is, incidentally, a generator of labelled traversal data — the thing humanoid navigation research is usually short of. Every campaign on this page wrote the same record shape, and the negatives are as labelled as the positives.

1,150

Generator samples archived

Across 15 preregistered campaigns, every one with exact launched-versus-returned accounting. 1,152 joint trajectories are stored and replayable, including every clip that failed a gate.

~7.7 s

Per clip, end to end

Generation is fast — batches of eight return in under a second on one RTX 5080. The cost is in scoring: a clearance profile sweeps the whole route against the collision model, which is what makes the labels trustworthy.

0.31

Yield at the staged optimum

Roughly one clip in three clears a 5 cm obstacle when the scene is staged to the prior's window. A dataset builder can either keep the winners or — more usefully — keep everything and label it, which is what the record shape above is for.

The honest caveat

These are kinematic labels against a simulation collision model. They are not physics-verified traversals and must not be reported as such until the tracker is in the endpoint. Any throughput figure quoted from this pipeline should name which tier it refers to.

Why the negatives are the valuable part A corpus of successful step-overs teaches a downstream policy what success looks like. A corpus that also carries where the prior tried and missed, by how much, and in which direction is a corpus you can learn a correction from. That is the shape this work argues for: scene, request, response, measured error, outcome — with the error signed and spatial, not a scalar reward.
