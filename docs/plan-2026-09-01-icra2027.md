# Scene2Motion-G1 plan of record — ICRA 2027

**Frozen:** 2026-09-01 after the repository/receipt audit.  **Submission deadline:**
2026-09-15 23:59 PST.  This document replaces the missing plan referenced by
`CLAUDE.md`; experiment receipts still outrank this plan.

## Decision now

The current paper is not yet ready to claim a deployed "stage, then select" method.
The strongest landed result is narrower and still useful:

> For the released ARDY-G1 Horizon52 checkpoint and its native conditioning interface,
> semantic step-over generation is not yet evidence of reliable obstacle-relative
> placement.  Scene2Motion measures the spatial addressability, exact whole-body
> clearance, tracking loss, and sampling cost of that interface.

Two tests can still change that conclusion and therefore precede paper polishing:

1. **Natural-motion tracking:** replay every v2 STEP clip from exp021 through SONIC and
   score the achieved state against an exact fixed obstacle.
2. **Prompt handoff:** switch WALK to STEP at later autoregressive boundaries.  ARDY's
   released interactive interface is designed to re-plan after prompt changes, so this
   must be tested before saying the event time is fixed or that staging is the only lever.

## Audit correction that changes E2

Exp021's historically published, post-hoc `0.312` (5 cm) and `0.266` (8 cm) rates use a
*placement-tolerance* endpoint: a clip counts if any scanned obstacle centre within
+/-0.25 m of the target clears. Those target-optimization values lack a committed derivation
script, and in any case are not the locked obstacle-centred endpoint.

At the post-hoc target `x = 1.2 m`, direct `BoxHeightProbe(1.2, 0.20)` replay gives:

| fixed box | exact hits | per clip | implied N=8 | N for 90% | N for 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 cm | 12 / 64 | 0.1875 | 0.810 | 12 | 15 |
| 8 cm | 11 / 64 | 0.1719 | 0.779 | 13 | 16 |

These are post-hoc calibration numbers from exp021, not prospective evidence.  The
findings page's `7-8 calls`, `95%/92%`, and staged demo are withdrawn as method results
until fresh seeds validate an exact fixed obstacle.  The demo places the obstacle at a
selected clip's measured lift and therefore demonstrates capability only.

## Campaign order and decision rules

### EXP-022A — exact fixed-obstacle SONIC bridge

Protocol: `docs/ramp-exp022-exact-tracking-protocol.md`.

- No new ARDY samples.  Consume all 64 archived exp021-v2 clips, not only winners.
- Lock `x = 1.2 m`, depth `0.20 m`, and graded heights
  `0.03/0.05/0.08/0.12/0.20/0.30 m`; also score the paired mid-route control at
  `x = 3.6 m`.
- Track all clips in two deterministic SONIC launches and replay every achieved qpos
  against the same geometry.  Isaac still has no box, so call the result
  "SONIC achieved-state replay," not physical obstacle contact.
- This is an exploratory/post-hoc bridge.  It answers whether natural prompt-elicited
  steps retain clearance under tracking and sizes the fresh campaign; it cannot validate
  the selected 1.2 m target.
- Fail closed on archive/key/schema drift, missing clips, tracker termination bookkeeping,
  dirty tracked source, changed checkpoint/config/source hashes, or incomplete launch
  accounting.

### EXP-023 — delayed prompt handoff

Protocol: `docs/ramp-exp023-prompt-handoff-protocol.md`.

- Fresh paired seed block beginning at 4500; v2 per-sample noise only.
- WALK history followed by either WALK or STEP at Horizon52-aligned switch frames.  The
  same pre-fork history and corresponding-window noise are paired; windows use non-repeated
  draws from one advancing per-seed stream so the old repeated-latent defect cannot recur.
- Include STEP-from-start and all-WALK references.  Route conditioning, total length,
  sampler settings, and suffix budget are identical.
- Primary endpoint: slope of realized event frame/position on prompt-onset frame/position,
  with missing events retained as failures.  Secondary: exact box clearance at the
  obstacle predicted from a fixed prompt-to-event latency.
- If the event follows prompt onset, timed prompting becomes the placement mechanism and
  replaces the current staging story.  If it remains tied to rollout origin, the negative
  timing claim becomes defensible.  No outcome-dependent arm expansion.

### EXP-022B — fresh exact-centre confirmation

Run only after EXP-022A and EXP-023 fix the mechanism and budget.

- Predeclare independent deployment trials/scenes and a common candidate budget; the
  provisional ceiling is N=16, not N=8.
- Derive one-shot and first-passing selection from the same STEP pool.  Score those exact
  clips at staged and mid-route obstacle centres; no extra samples for the unstaged
  rescoring control.
- Track every candidate once, then evaluate the preregistered reference-space selection
  on achieved states.  Never reselect using execution outcomes.
- Include a matched WALK floor with an explicit budget.  Do not include a packet arm
  unless donor construction, absolute-vs-residual choice, attrition, and all generator
  calls are charged in advance.
- Scene/deployment trial is the inference unit; candidate seeds are the within-trial
  resampling budget.

## Claims ledger for the paper

| claim | status now | evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| STEP prompt elicits whole-body-clearable motion in this checkpoint/configuration | landed, kinematic | exp021 archive + exact collision replay |
| STEP events concentrate early in the rollout | **provisional** | committed archive-to-event-frame analyzer with frozen conversion |
| Native rotation packets do not place the step and suppress amplitude | landed, kinematic | exp019 v7 + exp020, narrowly scoped |
| No native channel can move the event time | **open** | EXP-023 prompt-handoff; history/horizon scope stated |
| Staging at 1.2 m gives 90% in 7-8 calls | **withdrawn** | exact-centre fresh confirmation |
| Natural prompt steps survive SONIC | **open** | EXP-022A achieved-state replay |
| Scene2Motion performs physical obstacle traversal | unsupported | obstacle instantiated in Isaac or equivalent contact-rich execution |
| Best-of-N selection is novel | do not claim | position novelty in the addressability measurement and cost curve |
| Findings generalize across priors/behaviours | unsupported | Kimodo and another behaviour, or scope to one prior/behaviour |

## Paper shape if the two decisive tests land

Eight pages total, including references:

1. Motivation and claim scope.
2. Frozen prior -> exact scene-relative metric -> tracker pipeline.
3. Conditioning/addressability audit and controls.
4. Timing mechanism, including prompt handoff.
5. Exact fixed-obstacle cost and tracking-retention curves.
6. Fail-closed experimental process and limitations.

The paper must not lean on an external website: ICRA reviewers are not required to open
URLs.  Use an anonymous artifact mirror, remove names/affiliations/lab logos/faces and PDF
metadata, and keep the optional video under 180 seconds/20 MB.  Preserve a human-review
log for any AI-generated article text, figures, or code and follow the conference's
disclosure requirement.

## External evidence that constrains the framing

- ARDY documents prompt-change replanning and is kinematic, so delayed prompting is a
  required control: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08741>.
- TextOp demonstrates on-the-fly prompts on G1; Scene2Motion must differentiate through
  scene-relative clearance rather than the existence of prompt switching:
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07439>.
- SONIC's tracker metrics are too coarse to stand in for a 3-8 cm obstacle, which is why
  achieved world-space geometry remains the endpoint:
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07820>.
- Diffusion-noise optimization and PRESTO prevent broad "frozen means uncontrollable" or
  "best-of-N is novel" claims: <https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Karunratanakul_Optimizing_Diffusion_Noise_Can_Serve_As_Universal_Motion_Priors_CVPR_2024_paper.html>,
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16012>.
- Official ICRA 2027 call and double-anonymous rules:
  <https://2027.ieee-icra.org/contribute/call-for-icra-2027-papers-now-accepting-submissions/>,
  <https://www.ieee-ras.org/publications/rules-for-the-double-anonymous-review-process/>.
